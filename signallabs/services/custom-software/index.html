TideLine Strategies

Custom software built around your service business.

TideLine builds practical tools for owner-led service businesses when existing software leaves an important workflow gap. We define the work with the people doing it, then scope a build around the problem the business needs to solve.

When a custom build is worth considering

A standard product may handle most of the business while staff still rely on spreadsheets, repeated entry, or a string of messages to finish the job. Custom software can make sense when that gap is important enough to justify building and maintaining a tool.

We begin by checking the workflow and the existing systems. A better configuration or a simpler process may be a better first answer than a new application.

What we build around

  • Customer intake and follow-up handoffs.
  • Scheduling and staff coordination.
  • Operational reporting and shared records.
  • Admin workflows that connect the tools the team already uses.

The right interface depends on who uses it, which devices they work on, and what must happen when information is incomplete.

Define a useful first release

A first release should solve a named workflow for a named group of users. We agree the scope, permissions, data sources, and acceptance checks before treating a feature list as a plan.

Testing should follow the real task: can the staff member complete the work, can the owner review the result, and can someone recover when a step fails? A working demo helps answer a different question from a verified live workflow.

Ownership and handoff

You own the accounts, data, and documentation. The build should fit the existing business rather than leave the team dependent on a collection of disconnected tools.

Before selecting an integration, we check what access is available and what the provider supports. Ongoing hosting, maintenance, usage costs, and responsibility for support belong in the scope discussion.

Class-based operations are a starting lane

TideLine’s strongest operating context includes gymnastics, cheer, dance, wellness, and appointment-heavy service teams. WonPlace is the class-operations product featured on our site.

The same diagnostic-first method applies to other owner-led service teams. A new project is scoped around its actual workflow; a product preview is not a promise that every shown feature belongs in your build.

How much does a custom software project cost?

TideLine scopes custom work around the workflow, integrations, permissions, and ongoing support it needs. Share the problem and existing tools first so the team can discuss scope and pricing; there is no published one-size-fits-all build price.
